Making privacy visible in a local-first file manager

overview

role
Product designer
team
1 engineer
1 designer
skills
Product, UX
timeline
2024–2025
client
Bineon

Most file tools, and most AI assistants, send your files to a data center. On My Disk doesn’t — the files and the assistant both live on your device. The design problem was making that believable: privacy is invisible by default, so the interface had to show people where their data was at every point.

impact

Made file privacy and storage more understandable, with usability testing showing promising results — 0 of 26 participants described their files as being stored on a remote server in an open-ended question, compared with 2 of 3 in the initial study.

the problem

When testing a previous version of the UI with three participants, every participant but one assumed their files were sitting on someone else’s server.

the challenge

Make file storage, privacy status, and access permissions understandable at a glance, without compromising the simplicity of the interface.

my role

I was the sole product designer, responsible for end-to-end UX and UI design. Working alongside one engineer, I led the research, from defining research questions and recruiting participants to moderating sessions and analyzing findings. I developed the design system, prototyped key interactions, and delivered the final visual design.

key decisions

key decision 01

Visual indicators for file privacy

Users were confused about where their files were located and who had access to them. I made the distinction between public and private files visible at a glance through status indicators with color and visual cues.

key decision 02

Control through transparent onboarding and settings

In a research survey of 27 participants, 15 named control over their data as their most important factor when considering AI search, making it the most common pick. This is why the onboarding asks users to pick an AI model, choose whether to run it locally, or opt out of AI features entirely. Of course, they can later change their decision in the settings.

key decision 03

Natural-language search and filtering, at no cost to privacy

In the same survey, when asked what makes files hard to find, participants most often named memory reliance and confusion about directory structure. This is why I placed natural language search and recent files as the prominent sections of the dashboard, and added clear copy explaining that users can search with LLM without sending their files to a server for processing.

outcome

A second round tested the redesign with 26 new participants.

  • In an open-ended question, no participant described the file as being on a remote server.

    The question allowed a folder-path answer, yet five correctly identified the USB drive as its storage location. In the initial study, two out of three participants believed their files were uploaded online, suggesting an improvement, though the small initial sample limits the comparison.

  • 16 of 26 correctly identified a private file as private.

  • 21 of 26 set custom privacy settings when sharing a link, and all 21 made them less permissive than the default.

    This points to a preference for greater control over who can access shared files and for how long.

  • 22 of 26 shared a file through the three-dot menu rather than the link icon button, which is far more visually prominent.

    The affordance I designed to be obvious was not the one people reached for.

next steps

  1. Close the comprehension gap.

    Ten participants still misread a private file, indicating there is more work to do in communicating privacy.

  2. Improve visual cues.

    Most people chose to go through a longer route to share their file, rather than through a prominent ‘Link’ icon. The icon’s meaning, placement, and visibility should be investigated, with alternative designs and positions tested.

  3. Use closed-ended questions to measure comprehension.

    Add structured questions to test whether users understand where their files are stored and who can access them, making the results easier to compare across participants.
